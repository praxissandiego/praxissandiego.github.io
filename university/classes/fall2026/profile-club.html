Course Description

Who actually runs the world, and what are they like up close? The best magazine profiles answer that better than almost anything: a skilled writer spends months with one person and comes back with their habits, contradictions, ambitions and blind spots. Profile Club reads one of those each week.

We'll pick people with real influence today who are likely to matter even more in the years ahead, and we'll range widely: business and tech, politics and government, science and medicine, academia and culture. Think of an AI lab founder one week, a head of state the next, then a scientist whose work could change how we live. The reading list is partly yours: everyone who signs up is invited to suggest profiles they'd like to read with the group.

How a session works:

  • Grab lunch. Bring your own or something to share; we'll eat on the patio.
  • Read together. We read the week's profile at the same time. Profiles vary in length, but most take 30–60 minutes to read. Nobody has to prep, and nobody is behind.
  • Talk. An open-ended, unhurried discussion, usually about 90 minutes: what surprised you, what you'd ask this person, whether you trust them, and what their rise says about the world we're in. Most sessions run two hours or more.

If you enjoyed Essay Club, this is a natural next step: same lively discussion, but about the people behind the ideas. No background needed, just curiosity about people and power.

An online option is available for anyone who can't make it in person.
